## Basic Theory of N₂ Absorption Spectrum
Molecular nitrogen, N₂, is a homonuclear diatomic molecule. When electromagnetic radiation passes through N₂ gas, certain wavelengths are absorbed because the photon energy matches the energy difference between allowed molecular energy states. The resulting variation of absorption with wavelength is called the N₂ absorption spectrum.<img width="468" height="70" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/98605273-cb95-40a3-b05d-947a2528e4b4" />
## 1.1 Photon Absorption

Absorption of a photon promotes the molecule from an initial electronic state \(i\) to an excited state \(f\) according to

\[
\mathrm{N_2(X^1\Sigma_g^+)} + h\nu
\rightarrow
\mathrm{N_2^*}.
\]

The absorbed photon energy is

\[
E_\gamma = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda},
\]

where \(h\) is Planck's constant, \(c\) is the speed of light, \(\nu\) is the photon frequency, and \(\lambda\) is the photon wavelength.

Energy conservation requires

\[
E_f-E_i
=
\frac{hc}{\lambda_{\mathrm{abs}}},
\]

where \(E_i\) and \(E_f\) are the initial and final molecular energy levels, respectively, and \(\lambda_{\mathrm{abs}}\) is the wavelength of the absorbed photon.

Therefore, the absorption wavelength corresponding to a molecular transition is

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{\mathrm{abs}}
=
\frac{hc}{E_f-E_i}
}
\]

This relation provides the fundamental connection between the molecular energy-level structure of \(N_2\) and its absorption spectrum.
